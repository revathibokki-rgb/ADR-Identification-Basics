# 6. Severity / Intensity Assessment

Severity describes the intensity of the reaction and is separate from regulatory seriousness.

### Classification

**Important:** Severe intensity does not itself make an event serious. In this case, the event is serious because hospitalisation occurred.

# 14. Pharmacovigilance Case Narrative

A 62-year-old male with a history of hypertension and dyslipidaemia was started on atorvastatin 40 mg orally once daily for hyperlipidaemia. Approximately 21 days after initiation, he developed bilateral muscle pain, tenderness and weakness involving the thighs and calves. Laboratory investigation demonstrated a creatine kinase concentration approximately eight times the upper limit of normal. The patient required hospitalisation for clinical evaluation and management. Atorvastatin was discontinued. Following withdrawal, the patient's muscle symptoms improved and CK levels decreased. No convincing alternative cause was identified from the information available. Rechallenge was not performed. At the time of follow-up, the patient was recovering.

Based on the temporal relationship, recognised association between statin therapy and muscle-related adverse reactions, absence of a convincing alternative explanation, and positive dechallenge, the causal relationship was assessed as **Probable/Likely** using a WHO-UMC-style qualitative approach. The event was classified as **serious due to hospitalisation** and **severe in intensity**. Expectedness should be confirmed against the current applicable reference safety information.
