# ADR Identification Basics

## 1. Case Title

**Amoxicillin/Clavulanate-Induced Maculopapular Rash in an Adult Patient**

> **Note:** This is a hypothetical case created for pharmacovigilance learning and internship submission purposes.

---

## 2. Patient Profile

| Parameter                    | Details                             |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Patient ID                   | ADR-001                             |
| Age                          | 28 years                            |
| Sex                          | Female                              |
| Weight                       | 54 kg                               |
| Medical history              | No significant past medical history |
| Known drug allergies         | None reported                       |
| Diagnosis                    | Acute bacterial sinusitis           |
| Date of treatment initiation | Day 1                               |

---

## 3. Medication History

The patient was diagnosed with acute bacterial sinusitis and prescribed:

**Amoxicillin + Clavulanic acid 625 mg orally, three times daily.**

No other prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, herbal products or supplements were being taken during this period.

The patient had no previous documented allergy to penicillins or other beta-lactam antibiotics.

---

## 4. Description of the Suspected ADR

On **Day 4 of treatment**, the patient developed an itchy, red, maculopapular rash initially involving the trunk and subsequently extending to both upper limbs.

The patient reported:

* Generalised itching
* Red maculopapular lesions
* Mild skin discomfort

There was **no facial swelling, difficulty breathing, wheezing, hypotension, mucosal involvement, blistering or skin peeling**.

The patient did not report fever or any new food, cosmetic, personal-care product or other medication exposure.

Skin rash is a recognised adverse reaction associated with amoxicillin/clavulanate therapy.

---

## 5. Timeline of Events

| Day       | Event                                                                        |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Day 1     | Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625 mg TID initiated                                 |
| Day 2     | No adverse symptoms reported                                                 |
| Day 3     | No adverse symptoms reported                                                 |
| Day 4     | Pruritic maculopapular rash developed over trunk and upper limbs             |
| Day 4     | Suspected drug-related rash identified; amoxicillin/clavulanate discontinued |
| Day 5–6   | Rash and itching gradually decreased                                         |
| Day 7     | Rash substantially resolved                                                  |
| Follow-up | No recurrence reported                                                       |

---

## 6. Suspected Drug

**Primary suspected drug:** Amoxicillin

**Drug combination:** Amoxicillin + clavulanic acid

**Drug class:** Beta-lactam antibacterial / penicillin antibiotic

### Reason for suspicion

Amoxicillin is considered the primary suspected component because cutaneous hypersensitivity reactions are recognised with penicillin-class antibiotics. The temporal relationship between administration and development of the rash, followed by improvement after discontinuation, supports a drug-related reaction.

---

## 7. ADR Identification

**Adverse Drug Reaction:** Maculopapular skin rash associated with amoxicillin/clavulanate.

**Type of reaction:** Suspected hypersensitivity-type cutaneous reaction.

**Type A/B classification:** Type B (bizarre/unpredictable) reaction.

**Seriousness:** Non-serious in this hypothetical case.

**Expectedness:** Expected/listed adverse reaction because skin rash is included among reported adverse reactions of amoxicillin/clavulanate.

**Outcome:** Recovered after withdrawal of the suspected drug.

---

## 8. Assessment of Drug-Relatedness

Several factors support a relationship between the medication and the observed reaction:

### A. Temporal relationship

The rash developed approximately three days after initiation of amoxicillin/clavulanate, providing a plausible temporal association.

### B. Known association

Skin rash and urticaria are recognised adverse reactions associated with amoxicillin/clavulanate.

### C. Absence of alternative causes

The patient had no newly introduced medicines, foods, cosmetics or other relevant exposures.

### D. Positive dechallenge

The suspected medication was discontinued and the rash gradually resolved.

### E. Rechallenge

Rechallenge was **not performed** because deliberately re-exposing a patient to a suspected allergenic drug would not be appropriate merely to confirm causality.

The WHO-UMC system considers a reasonable temporal sequence, lack of a better alternative explanation and a clinically reasonable response after withdrawal as features supporting a **Probable/Likely** causality assessment.

| Naranjo criterion                                     | Assessment                   | Score |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----: |
| Previous conclusive reports of this reaction?         | Yes                          |    +1 |

| Reaction improved after discontinuation?              | Yes                          |    +1 |
| Reaction on readministration?                         | Not performed                |     0 |
| Alternative causes identified?                        | No                           |    +2 |
| Reaction with placebo?                                | Not performed                |     0 |

| Previous similar reaction?                            | No history                   |     0 |

**Total Naranjo score: 7**

### Interpretation

**Causality: Probable**

The score supports a probable relationship between amoxicillin/clavulanate and the observed maculopapular rash.

---

## 10. Management

The suspected antibiotic was discontinued after the appearance of the rash.

The patient was assessed for features suggesting a severe hypersensitivity reaction, including:

* Facial or airway swelling
* Respiratory difficulty
* Hypotension
* Mucosal lesions
* Blistering
* Skin peeling
* Systemic symptoms

No such features were present in this case.

Symptomatic treatment with an appropriate antihistamine may be considered for troublesome itching under medical supervision.

The prescribing information recommends discontinuation when hypersensitivity reactions occur and advises close monitoring if a rash progresses because severe cutaneous adverse reactions can occur.

---

## 11. Monitoring Parameters

The following parameters should be monitored:

* Progression or resolution of skin rash
* Severity of itching
* Development of urticaria
* Facial or tongue swelling
* Respiratory difficulty
* Blood pressure if systemic symptoms develop
* Mucosal involvement
* Blistering or skin peeling
* Fever or other systemic manifestations

---

## 12. Preventive Measures

The suspected reaction should be documented in the patient's medical record.

The patient should be advised to inform healthcare professionals about the suspected reaction before receiving future beta-lactam antibiotics.

Re-exposure to the suspected drug should not be attempted without an appropriate clinical indication and specialist assessment.

## 14. Conclusion

This hypothetical case demonstrates the identification and assessment of a suspected adverse drug reaction following amoxicillin/clavulanate therapy. The development of a maculopapular rash after initiation of treatment, absence of significant alternative causes and improvement following withdrawal support a probable drug-related reaction. Proper recognition, documentation, assessment and monitoring of ADRs are important components of pharmacovigilance practice.
