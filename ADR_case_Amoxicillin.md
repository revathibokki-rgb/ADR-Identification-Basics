# ADR Identification Basics

## Amoxicillin/Clavulanate-Induced Maculopapular Rash

**Task:** ADR Identification Basics
**Case Type:** Hypothetical pharmacovigilance case created for internship learning

---

## 1. Patient Profile

| Parameter            | Details                             |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Patient ID           | ADR-001                             |
| Age                  | 28 years                            |
| Sex                  | Female                              |
| Weight               | 54 kg                               |
| Medical history      | No significant past medical history |
| Known drug allergies | None reported                       |
| Diagnosis            | Acute bacterial sinusitis           |

---

## 2. Medication History

The patient was diagnosed with acute bacterial sinusitis and prescribed **amoxicillin + clavulanic acid 625 mg orally three times daily**.

The patient was not taking any other prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, herbal products or supplements.

There was no previous documented allergy to penicillin or other beta-lactam antibiotics.

---

## 3. ADR Description

On **Day 4 of treatment**, the patient developed an itchy, red maculopapular rash initially involving the trunk and later extending to both upper limbs.

### Symptoms observed

* Generalised itching
* Red maculopapular skin lesions
* Mild skin discomfort

The patient did **not** experience:

* Difficulty breathing
* Wheezing
* Facial or tongue swelling
* Hypotension
* Mucosal involvement
* Blistering
* Skin peeling

No new food, cosmetic product or other medication had been introduced before the reaction.

---

## 4. Timeline

| Day     | Event                                        |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Day 1   | Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625 mg TID initiated |
| Day 2   | No adverse symptoms                          |
| Day 3   | No adverse symptoms                          |
| Day 4   | Pruritic maculopapular rash developed        |
| Day 4   | Suspected drug discontinued                  |
| Day 5–6 | Rash and itching gradually decreased         |
| Day 7   | Rash substantially resolved                  |

---

## 5. Suspected Drug

**Suspected drug:** Amoxicillin

**Combination product:** Amoxicillin + clavulanic acid

**Drug class:** Penicillin antibacterial / beta-lactam antibiotic

Amoxicillin is considered the primary suspected drug because cutaneous hypersensitivity reactions are recognised with penicillin-class antibiotics.

---

## 6. ADR Identification

**Adverse Drug Reaction:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate-associated maculopapular skin rash.

**Reaction classification:** Suspected hypersensitivity-type cutaneous reaction.

**Type A/B classification:** Type B – bizarre/unpredictable.

**Seriousness:** Non-serious.

**Dechallenge:** Positive.

**Rechallenge:** Not performed.

**Outcome:** Recovered.

---

## 7. Assessment of Drug-Relatedness

### Temporal relationship

The reaction developed approximately three days after initiation of amoxicillin/clavulanate, providing a plausible temporal relationship.

### Known association

Skin rash is a recognised adverse reaction associated with amoxicillin/clavulanate.

### Alternative causes

No new medicines, foods, cosmetics or other relevant exposures were identified.

### Dechallenge

The suspected drug was discontinued and the rash gradually resolved.

### Rechallenge

Rechallenge was not performed because intentional re-exposure to a suspected allergenic drug would not be appropriate merely to confirm causality.

**Naranjo interpretation: Probable**

The score supports a probable relationship between amoxicillin/clavulanate therapy and the observed skin reaction.

---

## 9. WHO-UMC Causality Assessment

The case has:

* A reasonable temporal relationship between drug exposure and reaction
* No convincing alternative explanation
* Improvement following withdrawal of the suspected drug
* No rechallenge

### Causality Assessment: Probable/Likely

---

## 10. Management

The suspected antibiotic was discontinued following identification of the rash.

The patient was assessed for signs of severe hypersensitivity, including airway swelling, respiratory difficulty, hypotension, mucosal involvement, blistering and skin peeling. None were present in this case.

Symptomatic treatment for itching may be considered under appropriate medical supervision.

The patient was monitored until the rash substantially resolved.

---

## 11. Monitoring Parameters

The following parameters should be monitored:

* Progression or resolution of rash
* Severity of itching
* Development of urticaria
* Facial or tongue swelling
* Respiratory difficulty
* Blood pressure if systemic symptoms develop
* Mucosal involvement
* Blistering or skin peeling
* Fever or systemic symptoms

---

## 12. Preventive Measures

The suspected drug reaction should be documented in the patient's medical record.

The patient should be advised to inform healthcare professionals about the suspected reaction before receiving future antibiotics.

Re-exposure to the suspected drug should not be attempted solely for confirmation.

## 14. Conclusion

This hypothetical case demonstrates the identification and assessment of a suspected adverse drug reaction following amoxicillin/clavulanate therapy. The development of a maculopapular rash after initiation of treatment, absence of significant alternative causes and improvement following withdrawal support a probable drug-related reaction. Proper recognition, documentation, causality assessment and monitoring of ADRs are important components of pharmacovigilance practice.
