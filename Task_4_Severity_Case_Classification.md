# Task 4 – Severity & Case Classification

## Objective
To classify adverse drug reactions (ADRs) according to **seriousness, severity, and expectedness**, and to assess their clinical impact using standard pharmacovigilance principles.

## Assessment Principles

### Seriousness
An ADR is considered **serious** when it results in one or more of the following:
- Death
- Life-threatening condition
- Hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation
- Persistent or significant disability/incapacity
- Congenital anomaly/birth defect
- Other medically important condition requiring intervention to prevent a serious outcome

**Important:** Seriousness is a regulatory outcome and is not the same as intensity/severity.

### Severity / Intensity
Severity describes how intense the reaction is clinically:
- **Mild:** Minimal symptoms; little or no intervention required.
- **Moderate:** Symptoms require medical management or interfere with normal activities.
- **Severe:** Marked symptoms or substantial impairment, potentially requiring intensive treatment.

### Expectedness
An event is **expected** when its nature, severity, specificity, or outcome is consistent with the applicable reference safety information/product information. An event is **unexpected** when it is not consistent with that information or is more specific/severe than described.

## Case 1 – Mild Headache

**Patient:** 30-year-old female  
**Suspected drug:** Paracetamol 500 mg  
**Indication:** Fever  
**Event:** Mild headache

### Clinical assessment
The patient reported a mild headache during treatment. She remained able to perform normal activities and did not require additional medical intervention or hospitalisation.

- **Severity:** Mild
- **Seriousness:** Non-serious
- **Expectedness:** Expected, if consistent with the applicable product information
- **Clinical impact:** Minimal

### Classification
**Mild, Non-serious, Expected**

## Case 2 – Severe Nausea Requiring Treatment

**Patient:** 45-year-old male  
**Suspected drug:** Metformin  
**Indication:** Type 2 diabetes mellitus  
**Event:** Severe nausea and vomiting requiring antiemetic treatment and temporary interruption of therapy

### Clinical assessment
The patient experienced marked gastrointestinal symptoms that interfered with daily activities and required medical treatment. He was not hospitalised and did not develop a life-threatening condition.

- **Severity:** Severe
- **Seriousness:** Non-serious
- **Expectedness:** Expected, as gastrointestinal adverse effects are recognised with metformin
- **Clinical impact:** Significant temporary impairment requiring treatment

### Classification
**Severe, Non-serious, Expected**

**Note:** A severe reaction is not automatically a serious adverse event. Seriousness depends on regulatory seriousness criteria.

## Case 3 – Anaphylaxis Requiring Hospitalisation

**Patient:** 27-year-old female  
**Suspected drug:** Amoxicillin  
**Indication:** Bacterial infection  
**Event:** Anaphylaxis with hypotension, respiratory difficulty and emergency hospitalisation

### Clinical assessment
The patient developed an acute hypersensitivity reaction requiring emergency treatment and hospitalisation.

- **Severity:** Severe
- **Seriousness:** Serious – required hospitalisation and was potentially life-threatening
- **Expectedness:** Expected if anaphylactic/hypersensitivity reactions are included in the applicable product safety information; otherwise it would be unexpected
- **Clinical impact:** High; required emergency medical intervention

### Classification
**Severe, Serious, Expected* / Unexpected depending on the applicable reference safety information**

\*Expectedness must always be determined against the correct current reference safety information for the product and indication.

## Case 4 – Drug-Induced Liver Injury Requiring Hospitalisation

**Patient:** 52-year-old male  
**Suspected drug:** Isoniazid  
**Indication:** Tuberculosis treatment  
**Event:** Marked elevation of liver enzymes with jaundice and hospitalisation

### Clinical assessment
The patient developed clinically significant hepatic injury requiring admission for monitoring and treatment.

- **Severity:** Severe
- **Seriousness:** Serious – required hospitalisation
- **Expectedness:** Depends on whether the reported hepatic injury pattern/severity is described in the applicable reference safety information
- **Clinical impact:** High

### Classification
**Severe, Serious, Expectedness to be determined from the applicable reference safety information**

## Case 5 – Permanent Disability Following Adverse Reaction

**Patient:** 63-year-old female  
**Suspected drug:** Anticoagulant therapy  
**Indication:** Prevention of thromboembolic events  
**Event:** Intracranial haemorrhage resulting in persistent neurological impairment

### Clinical assessment
The patient developed a major bleeding event that resulted in permanent neurological impairment.

- **Severity:** Severe
- **Seriousness:** Serious – resulted in significant/persistent disability
- **Expectedness:** Must be assessed against the applicable reference safety information
- **Clinical impact:** Very high

### Classification
**Severe, Serious, Expectedness to be determined from the applicable reference safety information**

## Case 6 – Non-serious Moderate Rash

**Patient:** 34-year-old male  
**Suspected drug:** Amoxicillin  
**Indication:** Bacterial infection  
**Event:** Localised pruritic maculopapular rash

### Clinical assessment
The patient experienced itching and rash that required an antihistamine but did not require hospitalisation, cause disability, or become life-threatening.

- **Severity:** Moderate
- **Seriousness:** Non-serious
- **Expectedness:** Expected if consistent with the applicable product information
- **Clinical impact:** Moderate and temporary

### Classification
**Moderate, Non-serious, Expected**

## Key Learning Points

1. **Seriousness and severity are different concepts.** A reaction may be severe in intensity but non-serious from a regulatory perspective.
2. **Hospitalisation, life-threatening events, death, significant disability, congenital anomalies, and other medically important events can make an ADR serious.**
3. **Expectedness cannot be determined solely from clinical severity.** It must be assessed against the applicable reference safety information.
4. A complete pharmacovigilance case assessment should consider the patient's clinical outcome, treatment required, seriousness criteria, and applicable product safety information.

## Conclusion
Severity describes the intensity of an adverse reaction, whereas seriousness determines whether the event meets established regulatory seriousness criteria. Expectedness is assessed by comparing the event with the applicable reference safety information. Correctly distinguishing these concepts is essential for consistent pharmacovigilance case processing and regulatory reporting.
